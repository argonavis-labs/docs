We've watched how people use Runner. These are the patterns that work.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). **Ask** stages an **Action Card** for review. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless a user-created Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Start with email and calendar

Don't connect everything on day one. Start with the two apps you use most:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card icon={<img src="/images/apps/gmail.svg" alt="Gmail" width="24" height="24" />} title="Gmail">
    Inbox triage, draft replies, follow-up tracking.
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<img src="/images/apps/google-calendar.svg" alt="Google Calendar" width="24" height="24" />} title="Google Calendar">
    Meeting prep, scheduling, conflict detection.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Those two give Runner enough context to handle your daily briefing, email triage, and scheduling. Once you're comfortable, add Slack, then your project tracker, then whatever else.

<Tip>
  **First-day setup:** Gmail + Google Calendar. That's it. You can add more anytime.
</Tip>

## One Project per context

Keep different parts of your life in separate Projects:

| Project | What lives there |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Work** | Board prep sessions, your pipeline Runner, the weekly review |
| **Personal** | Trip planning, the subscription audit, family logistics |
| **Side project** | Every session and Runner for the newsletter |

Each Project carries its own context, so Runner starts every session in it already knowing the background. Got work and personal Gmail? Connect both in **Connected Apps** with **Add account** and tell Runner which one to use: *"Reply from my work email."*

---

## Keep sending on Ask while you learn

Set the send tool to **Ask** and Runner stages an **Action Card** before anything goes out:

1. Ask Runner to draft the email or message
2. Read it, then make any changes
3. Ask Runner to send it
4. Approve or reject the **Action Card**

Later, you can move a specific send tool to **Allow**. That removes the review step, so do it on purpose.

## Start with one job, not ten prompts

A one-off request shows you what Runner can do. The bigger payoff comes when you turn repeated work into a clear system.

| Too vague | Ready to test |
|-----------|---------------|
| "Help with sales" | "Every weekday at 8am, find open proposals that have waited 3 business days without a reply." |
| "Keep an eye on invoices" | "Every Friday, match new vendor invoices in Drive against the active agreements folder and flag differences." |
| "Watch product metrics" | "Every weekday at 8:30am, check the three approved funnels in Mixpanel and report changes above 15%." |

Name the sources, schedule, rules, what needs your approval, and what the result should contain. Then click **Run now** and read the first result.

If you're not sure which job to pick, [start with the work tied to revenue, cost, or time](/tips/tips-and-tricks).

## Save preferences that should stick

Tell Runner what you want it to remember:

*"When you draft emails for me, keep them under 5 sentences. Use a professional but friendly tone. Always end with a clear next step. Remember this."*

Then check **Memory**. Once it's saved, future sessions can use it.

---

## Be specific with names and dates

Runner works across multiple apps, so precision helps:

| Vague | Clear |
|-------|-------|
| "Check that email" | "Check the email from Sarah Chen about the Q2 budget" |
| "Schedule something next week" | "Find a 30-minute slot on Tuesday or Wednesday afternoon" |
| "Update my tasks" | "Mark the API bug task as done and add a note that the fix shipped" |

---

## Review new Runners closely

Read the first few runs. Fix repeated mistakes in the saved prompt or Project context, not only in one run's session.

Once a Runner is reliable, review it monthly. Check recent runs, pause work you no longer need, and confirm the schedule and permissions still make sense.

## Your first week with Runner

Here's a good first week. Each step builds on the last.

- [ ] [Connect Gmail and Google Calendar](/get-started/connecting-accounts)
- [ ] [Build your Personal Assistant](/quickstart)
- [ ] Click **Run now** and read the full result
- [ ] [Find one repeated job](/tips/tips-and-tricks) tied to revenue, cost, time, or a customer commitment
- [ ] Name its sources, schedule, useful result, and what needs your approval
- [ ] [Create a Project](/get-started/projects) for its standing business rules
- [ ] [Create the Runner](/runners/create-your-own) with writes on **Ask**
- [ ] Read the first three runs and fix the saved prompt when the same problem appears twice
- [ ] Add the next Runner only after the first one earns its place
