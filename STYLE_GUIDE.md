# Writing Style Guide

This is the voice for all Runner docs, help content, and user-facing copy. The goal: it should feel like a real person wrote it, not a content team or an AI.

## Core Characteristics

**Conversational and direct.** Write like you're talking to someone, not at them. No corporate jargon.

**Practical, not preachy.** Show people how to do things. Don't lecture.

**Short lines for impact, longer lines for flow.** Mix them up. Uniform paragraph lengths are a dead giveaway that nobody human touched the page.

**Trust the reader.** Don't over-explain. If someone is reading the docs, they're already trying to figure it out.

## What to Do

- Write like you talk
- Use contractions ("you're", "it's", "don't")
- Keep sentences varied in length
- Use second person ("you") by default
- Bold for UI elements: Click **Settings**
- Code formatting for file names, commands, paths
- Be specific. "Every weekday at 8am" not "on a regular basis"
- When something is simple, say so and move on

## What to Avoid

- Em dashes. Use periods, commas, or line breaks instead. "Runner checks your apps. Then gives you a brief." not "Runner checks your apps — then gives you a brief."
- Corporate jargon ("leverage", "synergy", "circle back", "from day one", "set yourself up for success")
- Fake enthusiasm or cheerleading ("Runner nails it!", "game changer")
- "Period." as a standalone emphasis word
- Passive voice ("it was decided")
- Long dense paragraphs without breathing room
- Hiding behind complexity
- Overly polished transitions ("Here's what that looks like in practice:")
- Adding "knowledge workers" when you can just describe the people

## Paragraph Rhythm

Mix lengthy explanatory paragraphs with short, punchy lines:

Good:
> Runner checks your calendar, triages your inbox, and summarizes Slack activity. Then gives you a ranked brief of what matters before your first meeting.

Also good:
> Keeps things clean.

The short lines land harder because they're surrounded by flow.

## Section Headers

Direct and conversational. Not marketing taglines.

- "Runner doesn't suggest. It finishes." (good)
- "Stop asking. Start finishing." (too tagline-y)
- "What are you looking for?" (good)
- "Unlock Your Productivity Potential" (no)

## Card and Link Descriptions

Keep these tight. One line. Don't repeat the title.

- "Quick wins and features you might not have found yet." (good)
- "Quick wins and hidden features to speed up your day and maximize your productivity." (too much)

## Copy-Paste / Template Messages

When writing example messages users can copy-paste (referral messages, email templates, etc.), write them the way a real person texts or emails. No marketing polish.

- "Hey, I've been using this app called Runner..." (good)
- "I wanted to reach out and share an exciting productivity tool..." (no)

## Security and Trust Sections

Be direct and confident without being corporate. Say what you do and don't do. Don't add filler.

- "We don't train models on your data." (good)
- "Your data is never used to improve or train artificial intelligence models, ensuring complete privacy." (too much)

## Tone Scale

This is a docs/help site, not a blog. Keep the personality present but measured.

| Too dry | Just right | Too much |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| "Configure the automation parameters." | "Tell Runner what you want and when." | "Runner is SO excited to automate your life!" |
| "Refer to the installation documentation." | "Download Runner and open it." | "Let's gooo! Time to install!" |
