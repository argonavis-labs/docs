Runner lets you build a fleet of AI employees for your highest-value jobs.

One follows every proposal. One watches spend. One catches a client commitment before it slips. One gets your day in order before the first meeting.

Each Runner is one AI employee with a saved job, a schedule, named sources, and clear rules for when to bring work back to you.

They handle the repetition. You keep the judgment, relationships, taste, and decisions that only you can make.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). **Ask** stages an **Action Card** for review. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless a user-created Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Every job needs to earn its place

Don't add another Runner because the demo looks clever. Add one because its job does at least one of these:

- Drives or protects revenue.
- Removes a real cost.
- Saves enough time to matter.
- Keeps a customer promise from slipping.

The job should repeat. The sources should exist. You should be able to tell when it's done. And you should know what stays in your hands.

## A fleet built around real work

| AI employee | What they own | Why the job matters | You stay focused on |
|-------------|---------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Proposal follow-up** | Checks the CRM and email for proposals waiting on a reply, then prepares the next message | Protects revenue already in motion | The relationship and the close |
| **Lead response** | Finds untouched inquiries, reads the account context, and prepares the first response | Shortens the gap between interest and action | Qualification and the conversation |
| **Cost watch** | Checks invoices, renewals, and spend against the rules you set | Finds waste and exceptions before month-end | The financial decision |
| **Client delivery** | Finds blocked work, missing approvals, and promises without an owner | Protects margin and client trust | Tradeoffs and client relationships |
| **Executive brief** | Pulls the few revenue, cost, customer, and delivery decisions that need you | Gives you time and attention back | Leadership and judgment |

That's the lens for every use case in this section. You're not collecting things Runner *can* do. You're deciding which high-value jobs belong in the fleet.

<Info>
  Each Runner works independently. Runners can use the same Project context, but they don't automatically pass work or results to one another. You decide how their jobs fit together.
</Info>

## Start with your own day

Your first AI employee can be a Personal Assistant. They read Gmail and Calendar, then give you a useful plan every morning. The job is familiar, the result is easy to check, and you learn how schedules and permissions work.

Then move into the business. Add the AI employee who can protect the most revenue, remove the clearest cost, or save the most time.

Personal: *What needs my attention today?*

Business: *Which proposals have waited three business days without a reply, and what should we send next?*

The setup is the same. The business job has a clearer number attached.

## Keep the human work

Runner should handle repeatable checks and bring you anything that needs judgment or approval.

Keep these with a person:

- The relationship.
- The final high-stakes decision.
- Taste, strategy, and tradeoffs.
- Anything legal, financial, or hard to undo.
