The best automation isn't the cleverest one. It's the one sitting next to revenue, cost, or a customer promise.

Runner lets you build that automation by explaining the job in plain English. Connect the apps where the work lives. Tell Runner how you decide what matters. Choose what can happen directly and what needs an **Action Card**. Then put it on a schedule.

You can still chat with Runner. In fact, talking is how you build and improve the system. The value comes from the work happening again tomorrow without another prompt.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). **Ask** stages an **Action Card** for review. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless a user-created Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Start with one expensive job

A good first Runner is easy to explain:

- It happens every day, week, or month.
- It reads from the same few sources.
- It needs some judgment, not just a fixed if-then rule.
- Missing it costs time, money, or trust.

For a services business, that might be a proposal with no follow-up, a lead sitting untouched in the CRM, or a client deliverable waiting on an owner. For an operating team, it might be an invoice exception, a campaign spending above target, or a release problem nobody caught.

Start with one. Get it reliable. Then build the next.

## Start with your own day. Then move into the business

A Personal Assistant is a good first Runner. It teaches you how schedules, context, and permissions work with a familiar job: read Gmail and Calendar, then give you a plan for the day.

The bigger opportunity comes next. Take the same pattern and aim it at a workflow with a dollar sign attached.

- Personal: *What needs my attention today?*
- Business: *Which proposals have waited three business days without a reply, and what should we send next?*

Same product. Much more valuable outcome.

## What each page gives you

Each page starts with work worth automating, not a list of things to ask in a session. It answers five questions:

- What does it cost when this work gets missed?
- What should Runner check, and how often?
- What should a useful result contain?
- Which actions should wait for review?
- What should you build after the first Runner works?

If your role isn't here, use the same test: what repeats, what does it cost, and what happens when nobody owns it?
