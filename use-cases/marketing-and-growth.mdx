A campaign report can be perfectly accurate and still be useless.

Clicks went up. CPA went down. Great. Did any of it create pipeline? Is one campaign burning through the week's budget? Is a high-value lead still waiting for a response?

That's the work to put on a schedule.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). Keep budget changes, CRM updates, and posts on **Ask** while you test. Runner stages each write as an **Action Card**. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless the Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Start with spend and revenue

| Runner | What it checks | Useful result | Value |
|--------|----------------|---------------|-------|
| **Paid spend exception** | Ad results or spend sheet, targets, and CRM outcomes | Only campaigns outside your approved limits | Catches waste before month-end |
| **Campaign-to-pipeline review** | Campaign data and CRM opportunities | Campaigns tied to pipeline, plus gaps in the source data | Moves the conversation past clicks |
| **Lead aging check** | New marketing leads and CRM activity | Leads waiting without an owner or first response | Protects the demand you already paid for |
| **Content bottleneck check** | Content board, due dates, and owners | Work blocked, overdue, or missing a next step | Keeps production from stalling |
| **Weekly growth review** | Approved channel metrics and current experiments | What changed, what needs investigation, and who owns it | Replaces report assembly with decisions |

Runner can compare the sources and apply your rules. It shouldn't invent causality. A metric moving after a campaign launched doesn't prove the campaign caused it.

## Build this first: paid spend exception

```text
Create a Runner named Paid Spend Exceptions.

Every weekday at 8:00am in my timezone, review campaign performance in
[connected ad source or Google Sheet], our approved targets in [Google Sheet],
and pipeline outcomes in [HubSpot or Salesforce].

Only include a campaign when one of these is true:
- daily spend is more than 20% above its approved pace
- cost per qualified lead is more than 20% above target
- spend increased but the connected CRM shows no matching qualified leads
- required campaign or source data is missing

For each exception, show campaign, spend, target, variance, qualified leads or
pipeline shown in the CRM, and the exact source period. Separate facts from
possible explanations. Don't claim why a metric changed unless a source says it.

Recommend one thing for a person to inspect. Don't change budgets, pause ads,
post messages, or update the CRM. Draft a short team summary for my review.
If nothing is outside the rules, say that clearly and stop.

Before you create the Runner, show me the final name, prompt, schedule, and
timezone. Wait for my confirmation.
```

**A user-created Runner becomes active as soon as it's created.** Review its schedule and permissions, then click **Run now**.

Twenty percent is an example. Use a threshold your team already trusts.

## Keep the definitions in one place

Put the rules in a marketing Project:

- Approved targets and where the current numbers live.
- What counts as a qualified lead or pipeline opportunity.
- Naming conventions for channels and campaigns.
- Reporting windows and timezone.
- Changes Runner may recommend but must not make.

The ad source, CRM, and reporting sheet hold the live numbers. Project context holds the definitions. Previous run output doesn't carry forward automatically.

Run the first few checks with writes on **Ask**. If the same false positive appears twice, fix the rule in the saved prompt. Don't just correct one session and hope the next run knows.

## Then automate these

### Campaign-to-pipeline review

Run Monday morning. Match campaign and lead records using the identifiers your data actually provides. Show matched pipeline, unmatched records, and missing fields. Don't turn correlation into attribution.

### Lead aging check

Run each weekday morning. Find new marketing leads with no owner or first response, then stage an alert or CRM update. Stop when the source record shows the lead is handled.

### Content bottleneck check

Run each weekday morning. Find content due in the next seven days with no owner, blocked status, or overdue dependency. Draft the nudge, then let a person decide what moves.

### Weekly growth review

Run Friday afternoon. Pull the approved metrics, current experiments, spend exceptions, and pipeline changes into one short report. Flag missing data instead of smoothing over it.

## A report isn't the outcome

The goal is to catch wasted spend early, follow up on demand, and start the team meeting with a decision instead of spending 40 minutes building the report.

Build for that.
