An invoice can match last month's total and still be wrong.

The rate changed. The contract ended. The same invoice arrived twice. Someone notices three weeks later because the real work is buried across Gmail, Drive, and a tracking sheet.

Runner checks those sources on a schedule and brings you the exceptions. You still approve every payment.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). Keep messages, records, and approvals on **Ask** while you test. Runner stages each write as an **Action Card**. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless the Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Start with the expensive exceptions

| Runner | What it checks | Useful result | Boundary |
|--------|----------------|---------------|----------|
| **Invoice exception check** | Incoming invoices, agreements, and tracking sheet | Duplicates, rate changes, missing terms, and unmatched charges | Runner flags. A person approves or pays. |
| **Vendor renewal watch** | Contracts, renewal notices, and spend records | Renewals approaching with owner, amount, and decision date | Runner doesn't renew or cancel on its own. |
| **Receivables follow-up** | Open invoice list and customer email | Past-due accounts with a draft based on the customer thread | A person reviews the relationship before sending. |
| **Report input chase** | Reporting sheet and owner list | Missing sections and draft reminders | Escalation rules stay in the prompt. |
| **Close readiness** | Close checklist and available source files | Open items, missing evidence, and named blockers | Accounting judgment stays with finance. |

The safest first Runner doesn't move money. It tells you where to look.

## Build this first: invoice exception check

```text
Create a Runner named Invoice Exceptions.

Every Friday at 9:00am in my timezone, review vendor invoices received in the
last 7 days in [Gmail or Drive folder], active agreements in [Drive folder],
and the invoice tracking sheet in [Google Sheet].

For each invoice that isn't already marked reviewed in the tracking sheet,
extract the vendor, invoice number, invoice date, service
period, line items, total, currency, and payment terms shown in the source.
Compare those fields with the matching active agreement and tracking sheet.

Flag only:
- a possible duplicate invoice number or amount
- a rate, quantity, or total that doesn't match the agreement
- a charge outside the agreement dates or scope
- missing agreement, purchase reference, or required field
- payment terms that changed

Cite the invoice and agreement used for every flag. If a document is missing
or unreadable, say so. Don't approve, reject, pay, or edit an accounting
record. Return a table for review and a short list of questions for the owner.

Before you create the Runner, show me the final name, prompt, schedule, and
timezone. Wait for my confirmation.
```

**A user-created Runner becomes active as soon as it's created.** Review its schedule and permissions, then click **Run now**.

Run it against a small set first. Five invoices you already checked will tell you more than a perfect-looking prompt.

## Give it the rules

Put stable rules in an operations Project:

- Where current agreements and approved vendor records live.
- Required fields and matching tolerances.
- Owners for vendors, reports, and close items.
- Escalation timing.
- Actions Runner must never take without a person.

Keep invoice, contract, and checklist status in the source files. Each run starts in a separate session and doesn't automatically receive the last run's output.

Start every financial write on **Ask**. In many cases, the right permission is **Off**. Runner can do the collection and comparison without becoming the person who approves the result.

## Add the next check

### Vendor renewal watch

Run Monday morning. Find agreements renewing in the next 60 days, then show owner, current cost, notice deadline, and any renewal email. Draft the question. Don't renew or cancel.

### Receivables follow-up

Run twice a week. Read the approved open-invoice list and matching customer thread. Draft a follow-up only when the source says the invoice is past due and no payment or dispute is recorded.

### Weekly operating report

Run after the reporting deadline. Pull submitted numbers into the agreed format, identify missing inputs, and draft reminders for the owners. Don't invent a number to complete the page.

### Close readiness

Run each weekday morning. If the close checklist isn't active, stop. During close, list open items and missing support, then name the source or owner shown. Keep reconciliations and sign-off with finance.

## The useful result is a shorter exception list

Runner doesn't need to "run finance." It needs to take a long list and show only the items that deserve a human decision.

That's where the time and money come back.
