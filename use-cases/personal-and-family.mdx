Your personal life already has workflows. They're just living in your head.

Check the school email. Remember the dentist. Notice the subscription renewal. Look at the calendar before promising Saturday afternoon. None of it is hard. It just keeps coming back.

Personal work is a good place to build your first Runner because you know what right looks like.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). Keep messages, calendar changes, purchases, cancellations, and bookings on **Ask** while you test. Browser checkouts and submissions add a final confirmation before they're submitted.
</Note>

## Start with the work you repeat

| Runner | What it checks | Useful result |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| **Personal Assistant** | Gmail and Google Calendar | A short plan with priorities, meetings, conflicts, and commitments |
| **Open-loop check** | Sent email and upcoming calendar | People waiting on you and promises without a next step |
| **Sunday family preview** | Family calendar and relevant email | The week ahead, missing details, and conflicts to solve |
| **Renewal review** | Receipts, renewal notices, and your tracking sheet | Charges approaching, with amount and decision date shown in the source |
| **Trip research** | Calendar, preferences, and browser results | A comparison you can review before booking anything |

Start with the Personal Assistant. It gives you a useful result every morning while you learn how connected apps, schedules, and permissions work.

## Build this first: Personal Assistant

```text
Create a Personal Assistant Runner for me.

Every day at 9:00am in my timezone, review my connected Gmail and Google
Calendar. Give me a short plan with:

1. My three most important priorities.
2. Meetings that need preparation.
3. Commitments and open loops that need attention.
4. Schedule conflicts, deadlines, or important messages I may have missed.
5. One repeated piece of personal admin that may be worth automating.

Cite the email or event behind each item. If the source is unclear, say so.
Don't send messages, change my calendar, buy, book, or cancel anything. Draft
or suggest the next step for my review.

Before you create it, show me the final name, prompt, schedule, and timezone.
Wait for my confirmation.
```

**A user-created Runner becomes active as soon as it's created.** Review its schedule and permissions, then click **Run now**.

The full walkthrough is in [Quickstart](/quickstart), including Gmail and Calendar permissions, testing with **Run now**, and saving feedback where it lasts.

## Make the useful parts stick

Put stable preferences in [Personal Memory](/using-runner/memory):

- How early you want to hear about an event.
- Travel, food, and transportation preferences.
- Names and relationships you want Runner to understand.
- The tone you use for personal messages.

Put standing instructions in the saved prompt or a Personal Project. Keep live dates, replies, and charges in Calendar, Gmail, or the tracking sheet Runner reads.

Each run starts in a separate session. Last week's output doesn't automatically come along. If a fact should last, save it to Personal Memory. If a rule should apply every time, put it in the Runner's prompt.

## Build the next one

### Open-loop check

Run at 4:30pm each weekday. Look for messages you sent that still need a reply and commitments due soon. Draft the next follow-up, but don't send it.

### Sunday family preview

Run Sunday at 6pm. Read the next seven days of Calendar and relevant family email. List appointments, school events, travel, conflicts, and anything missing a time or place.

### Renewal review

Run on the first of each month. Find renewal notices and recurring receipts in email, then show the amount and renewal date stated in the source. A receipt doesn't prove whether you still use something, so ask before recommending a cancellation.

### Trip research

Run when you're planning, or on a schedule while prices matter. Compare options against the dates and preferences you give it. Runner shows the final browser purchase or booking action and asks before submitting.

## Learn here, then move up

A morning plan gives you time back. A renewal review can save a little money. Once those work, take the same structure into your business: clear sources, clear rules, clear review points, and a schedule.

That's how one useful assistant turns into several useful Runners.
