A full pipeline can still be a dead pipeline.

The problem usually isn't the number of records. It's the gaps: a new lead with no reply, a proposal with no follow-up, a deal with no next step, or a renewal nobody owns.

Those gaps repeat every week. That makes them good work for a Runner.

<Note>
  Writes follow your [tool permissions](/connections/tool-permissions). Keep CRM updates and email sending on **Ask** while you test. Runner stages each write as an **Action Card**. **Allow** acts without asking. **Off** blocks the tool unless the Runner has its own override.
</Note>

## Put revenue follow-through on a schedule

| Runner | Schedule | What it finds | Finish line |
|--------|----------|---------------|-------------|
| **New lead check** | 8am and 4pm each weekday | Inquiries with no owner or first response | Owner named and reply ready for review |
| **Proposal follow-up** | Every weekday morning | Sent proposals with no client reply | Specific follow-up staged, or a clear reason to stop |
| **Pipeline gap check** | Monday and Thursday | Open deals with no next step, stale activity, or overdue tasks | Short list with owner and next action |
| **Renewal watch** | Twice a week | Renewals approaching with open issues or no plan | Risk, owner, and next conversation named |
| **Sales report** | Friday afternoon or month-end | Won, lost, slipped, and stage changes | Report built from CRM data, with missing fields called out |

Don't automate "do sales." Automate one gap you can see and measure.

The 8am and 4pm lead check uses more of your plan. Start with the morning check until the second one is worth the cost.

## Build this first: pipeline gap check

```text
Create a Runner named Pipeline Gap Check.

Every Monday and Thursday at 8:00am in my timezone, review open opportunities
in [HubSpot or Salesforce] and the matching Gmail threads.

Include a deal when at least one of these is true:
- there is no next step or next-step date
- the next-step date has passed
- there has been no recorded activity for 7 days
- a proposal was sent at least 3 business days ago with no client reply

Exclude deals marked won, lost, paused, or do not contact.

For each deal, read the CRM record and latest email thread. Return the deal,
stage, value shown in the CRM, days since activity, owner, missing field, and
one recommended next step. If a follow-up is appropriate, draft it from the
real context and stage the send as an Action Card. Don't invent urgency,
pricing, product claims, or a deadline.

If no deals match, say that clearly and stop. Before you create the Runner,
show me the final name, prompt, schedule, and timezone. Wait for my confirmation.
```

**A user-created Runner becomes active as soon as it's created.** Review its schedule and permissions, then click **Run now**.

The result should be small enough to act on. Ten real gaps beat a 60-page pipeline export.

## Keep the state where Runner can read it

Put your sales rules in a Project:

- Ideal customer and disqualifiers.
- Deal stages and what each one requires.
- Follow-up timing and do-not-contact rules.
- Approved product language and pricing boundaries.
- Who owns leads, proposals, and renewals.

Keep deal status in the CRM and conversation status in email. The next run reads those sources again. It doesn't automatically receive the previous run's output.

Start with writes on **Ask**. Review the first ten email and CRM Action Cards. If a rule keeps needing correction, change the saved prompt or Project context. Don't leave the fix in one run's session.

## Add the next revenue check

### New lead response

Run at the start and end of each workday. Find new inbound leads with no owner or reply, read the available company and CRM context, and stage a short first response. Stop as soon as the record or thread shows somebody handled it.

### Proposal follow-up

Run each weekday morning. Check proposals sent 3 to 14 business days ago. Draft the next message only when the client hasn't replied and the opportunity is still open.

### Renewal watch

Run twice a week. Find renewals in the next 60 days, then gather recent email, open issues, usage data that is actually connected, and the named owner. Surface missing plans early.

### Weekly sales report

Run Friday afternoon. Pull what changed from the CRM, not from memory. Show won, lost, slipped, stale, and missing data. Keep forecast judgment with the sales owner.

## Measure the gap, not the output

Don't count how many emails Runner drafted. Count leads that got a first response, proposals that received a next step, and open deals that stopped sitting without an owner.

That's the revenue work.
