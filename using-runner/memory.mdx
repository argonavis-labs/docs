---

Tell Runner something once and it's remembered. Not the "oh I forgot everything from yesterday" kind of memory. Real memory. The kind where it learns how you write, who matters to you, and how you like things done, and gets smarter the more you use it.

- *"Keep my email drafts to three sentences max."*
- *"Sarah and James are always high priority."*
- *"I don't have a car. Transit options only."*

Say it in any session, on any device. Every future session already knows.

## See what Runner knows

Open **Memory** in your sidebar. Every memory is a document you can read, edit, or delete, organized in folders you can nest however you like. People, projects, preferences, your company.

Nothing is hidden. If Runner believes something wrong about you, open the memory and fix it, or just tell it: *"That's outdated. I moved teams in June."*

You can add memories by hand too. Click **New memory**, give it a title and a body, done. Useful for the stuff you'd otherwise repeat forever: your travel preferences, your kids' school schedule, how you like reports formatted.

---

## Memory keeps itself clean

Memory that grows forever becomes a junk drawer. Runner does its own housekeeping:

**It tidies.** Hit **Organize** on the Memory page and Runner files stray memories into the right folders in the background. It also does this on its own every week, consolidating duplicates and keeping the structure sane.

**It stays fresh.** Runner periodically sweeps your connected apps, read-only, to keep its understanding of your world current. Who you're talking to, what's active, what's gone quiet. These sweeps only ever write to your memory. They never touch your apps.

---

## Feedback is memory

The fastest way to build great memory is to correct Runner like you'd correct a colleague:

> *"You recommended I drive there, but I don't have a car. Please remember to use transit options next time."*

That correction becomes memory. The next suggestion uses transit. This is the whole loop: feedback in, better output forever after.
