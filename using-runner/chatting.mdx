You can use Runner the same way you'd use ChatGPT or Claude or Gemini. Open a conversation, type what you need, and Runner responds.

The difference is that Runner is connected to your actual apps. So instead of just answering questions, it can read your email, check your calendar, send a Slack message, and draft a reply. It does things.

## Think employee, not chatbot

Runner isn't your therapist. It's not here to chat back and forth about ideas (though it can). Think of it more like an incredibly capable employee who learns how you work and gets better the more you use it.

You say: *"Check my inbox and draft replies to anything urgent."*

Runner does it.

You say: *"What's on my calendar tomorrow and who do I need to prep for?"*

Runner pulls it together.

You don't need to learn a special syntax or memorize commands. Just tell it what you need in plain language.

---

## Start a conversation

Click **New Chat** or use the keyboard shortcut. Type your request. That's it.

Every conversation lives in your current workspace, so Runner only sees the apps connected to that workspace. If you're in your Work workspace, it sees your work email. If you're in Personal, it sees your personal email.

---

## What you can ask

Runner can do anything your connected apps can do. A few categories to give you the idea:

### Questions about your stuff
- *"What's on my calendar today?"*
- *"Do I have any emails from Sarah this week?"*
- *"What tasks are assigned to me in Linear?"*

### Getting things done
- *"Draft a reply to the last email from Jordan"*
- *"Send a message to #general on Slack saying standup is pushed to 11"*
- *"Create a task in Linear for the homepage redesign"*

### Pulling things together
- *"Give me my morning briefing: calendar, priority emails, and Slack mentions"*
- *"Summarize the email thread about the Q2 budget"*
- *"What did I miss in Slack overnight?"*

### Thinking through stuff
- *"Help me write a cold outreach email to this company"*
- *"Review this budget and flag anything unusual"*
- *"What should I prioritize this week based on my calendar and tasks?"*

The more apps you connect, the more Runner can do.

## It remembers how you work

Tell Runner your preferences once and it applies them going forward:

*"When you draft emails for me, keep them short. Three sentences max. Friendly but direct."*

*"I prefer bullet points over long paragraphs."*

*"Always check my calendar before suggesting meeting times."*

You don't need to repeat yourself. Runner remembers.

---

## Conversations stay in your workspace

Each conversation belongs to the workspace it was created in. Your work conversations stay in Work. Your personal ones stay in Personal. Nothing crosses over.

You can search across all your conversations with `Cmd+F` (Mac) or `Ctrl+F` (Windows/Linux).

---

## Voice input

Click the mic icon or use the keyboard shortcut to talk instead of type. Useful when you're multitasking or thinking out loud.

*"Hey Runner, check my calendar for tomorrow and tell me if I have any conflicts."*

Works exactly like typing, just faster when your hands are busy.

---

## Attach files

Drag a file into the conversation for extra context. PDFs, spreadsheets, images, text files.

*[Attached: proposal-v2.pdf]*

*"Review this proposal and give me the three biggest concerns."*

---

## A few tips

- **Be specific.** "Check the email from Sarah about the Q2 budget" beats "check my email."
- **Chain requests.** "Check my calendar, prep for my meetings, and draft a Slack standup" all in one message. Runner handles it.
- **Ask why.** If Runner does something you didn't expect, ask it to explain. It will, and you can correct it.
- **Start with questions.** When you're new, ask Runner what it can see before telling it what to do. Builds trust fast.
