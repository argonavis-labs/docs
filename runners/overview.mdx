A Runner is a prompt that runs on a schedule. That's the whole idea, and it changes everything.

Write what you want done, pick when it should happen, and Runner does it on a dedicated virtual computer in the cloud. Every day at 8am. Weekdays at 5pm. The first of the month. Your laptop can be closed. You can be on a flight. The work happens anyway.

Run one at 5am and your morning brief is in your inbox before you wake up.

## Why Runners are different from old-school automations

**They keep context between runs.** A Runner isn't a fresh robot every morning. It remembers what it found yesterday, what you told it, and what's still open. Your follow-up tracker knows which follow-ups it already flagged.

**They can reach you.** Ask a Runner to email you its results and it will. It only sends email when you've explicitly asked it to, and you control the daily limit in **Settings, then Notifications**.

**They propose, you approve.** When a Runner wants to take an action, like sending a reply or updating your CRM, it stages an **Action Card**: a preview you can approve, reject, or dismiss with one click. Unresolved cards can carry into the next run so nothing falls through.

**You control their tools.** Every Runner has its own **Tool permissions** section. Your inbox triage Runner can read email but never send it, while your Personal Assistant gets more rope. [How tool permissions work →](/connections/tool-permissions)

## Anatomy of a Runner

Every Runner has:

- **A prompt.** What it should do on each run. This is just plain language, the same way you'd type it in a session.
- **A schedule.** Daily, weekdays, weekly, monthly, or a custom cron expression, in your timezone. The details page shows you exactly what the schedule means and when the next runs are.
- **An intelligence setting.** Auto, Normal, or Ultra, or pin a specific model. [More on models →](/using-runner/models)
- **A Project (optional).** Runners can live inside a Project and inherit its context. Your pipeline-review Runner belongs in your Sales project. [More on Projects →](/get-started/projects)
- **A run history.** Every run creates a session. Open the transcript of any run and see exactly what it did and why.

You can also give it an avatar. Mine's an animal. This matters more than you'd think, because you're going to end up talking to these things every day.

## Runs you can watch

Each run shows up in **Recent runs** on the Runner's page, marked **Scheduled** or **Manual**, with its status. Click **Open transcript** on any of them and read the whole session.

Want results right now instead of waiting for the schedule? Hit **Run now**.
